# Impact report: agent arm vs randomized holdout

_Generated by `python -m ott_agent report` from the replay, the pack's simulator and the outcome judge. Method: [doc/decisions.md D-044](../doc/decisions.md)._

## 1. Result

On the primary metric (share of viewers whose first goal succeeded, intention-to-treat), the agent arm reached **16.0 %** and the holdout **14.8 %**: a difference of **+1.1 pts [-2.8, +5.1]** (95 % interval). That is **no detectable effect (the 95 % interval includes zero)**.

Goal-independent check: any play in the 7 days after the first visit, agent 69.1 % vs holdout 67.3 %, +1.8 pts [-2.9, +6.5]: no detectable effect (the 95 % interval includes zero).

What this study can detect: with 657 and 647 judged viewers, the 95 % interval is about ±3.9 points wide, so a true effect smaller than that cannot be told apart from zero with one replay day.

## 6. Exposure

- Agent-arm viewers in the cohort: 758; a page was published at their first visit for 625.
- Of 681 agent viewers with a goal, **557** (81.8 %) actually saw one of our pages in the goal window.

- Secondary estimate, *effect on viewers who saw a page* = ITT ÷ seen rate = +1.4 pts [-3.4, +6.2]. This assumes a viewer who saw no page was not affected at all; it is shown for completeness and is not the headline.

## 7. Why "served vs not served" is misleading (shown for illustration only)

| Group | First goal succeeded | Any play within 7 days | Play days in the 14 days **before** the visit |
|---|---|---|---|
| Agent arm, saw our page | 96/537 = 17.9 % | 76.5 % | 2.00 |
| Agent arm, did not see our page | 9/120 = 7.5 % | 48.8 % | 1.26 |
| Holdout (randomized comparison group) | 96/647 = 14.8 % | 67.3 % | 1.87 |

Whether a viewer *sees* our page depends on the viewer: they must come back while the page is live, in the same dialect. Viewers who come back are, by definition, the more active ones; the last column shows they were already more active **before** the agent did anything. Comparing "saw a page" with "did not" (or with everyone else) therefore mixes the agent's effect with who those viewers already were: selection bias. Only the randomized comparison in sections 1–5 is free of it, because the arm was assigned by coin flip before the agent acted and every viewer counts in the arm they were assigned to.

**The same trap on our own replay.** The pilot report's comparison (served vs everyone else) computed on our data, next to the randomized result:

| Comparison | Group A | Group B | Difference (95 % interval) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Served (page published at the first visit) vs everyone else (**biased**) | 105/600 = 17.5 % | 96/704 = 13.6 % | +3.9 pts [-0.1, +7.9] |
| Agent arm vs holdout arm, randomized (**correct**) | 105/657 = 16.0 % | 96/647 = 14.8 % | +1.1 pts [-2.8, +5.1] |

The biased comparison estimates +3.9 pts [-0.1, +7.9], the randomized one +1.1 pts [-2.8, +5.1]. The biased estimate is larger because of who gets served, not because of what serving does.

## 8. Robustness across simulator seeds

Same published actions, re-simulated with other seeds and re-judged (the evaluators use their own seed).

| Seed | First goal succeeded: agent vs holdout | Difference (95 %) | Any play in 7 days: difference |
|---|---|---|---|
| default (this sample run) | 16.0 % vs 14.8 % | +1.1 pts [-2.8, +5.1] | +1.8 pts [-2.9, +6.5] |
| seed-a | 14.9 % vs 13.8 % | +1.2 pts [-2.7, +5.0] | +4.5 pts [-0.3, +9.3] |
| seed-b | 16.0 % vs 14.0 % | +2.0 pts [-1.9, +5.9] | +0.0 pts [-4.8, +4.8] |
| seed-c | 13.4 % vs 13.7 % | -0.3 pts [-4.0, +3.4] | +3.5 pts [-1.3, +8.2] |

Across 8 intervals (primary and goal-independent metric, 4 seeds), 7 point estimates are positive and 0 interval(s) exclude zero. At 95 % confidence about one interval in twenty excludes zero by chance alone, so a single such interval is not evidence of an effect; consistent small positive estimates suggest at most a small effect that one replay day cannot confirm.

## 10. Limitations of this measurement

- One replay day; per-goal samples are small and their intervals wide.
- 14-day goals (G06, G09) are mostly inconclusive: the data ends on their deadline.
- Outcomes come from the pack's simulator; its behaviour model, not real viewers, decides the effect size.
- The cohort excludes viewers whose only app opens were stale or duplicates (same rule in both arms).
